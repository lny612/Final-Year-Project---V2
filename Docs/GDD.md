# The Wand Atelier — Game Design Document
---

## 1. Overview

**Genre:** Fantasy crafting / deduction game
**Engine:** Unity 6 (6000.0.58f2) with Universal Render Pipeline (URP)
**Platform:** PC (Windows)
**Scope:** Originally a 6-week solo build; subsequent additions (7-day session, multi-ending, memo gate, expanded minigame, UI Toolkit migration) built on top of the original milestone schedule.

**Core fantasy:** You are a wand artisan. Customers arrive with a personality, magical school, and hidden need. Read their dossier, gather materials, perform a crafting ritual, and forge the wand that fits them. Better match + better craftsmanship = greater reputation.

---

## 2. Core Experience Pillars

1. **Deduction** — Dossier has a surface request and a hidden need in tension with the constraint. Skilled players read deeper.
2. **Creative flexibility** — No fixed recipes. Any wood+core can work if reasoning is sound. AI evaluates logic, not a lookup table.
3. **Crafting as ritual** — Tracing minigame x3 makes the player feel like a wizard performing magic, not clicking a button.

---

## 3. Daily Loop

A session runs **7 in-game days**, bookended by a Title screen and a multi-ending payoff. Rent is billed at the end of days 3 and 6.

```
Title  →  [Day 1 .. Day 7]  →  Ending
              │
              ▼ (one day = one wand order)
   Morning (letter)
   → Customer (dossier + memo)
   → Material (market)
   → Crafting (selection)
   → Minigame (tracing) ‖ Wand-gen pipeline (in parallel)
   → Evaluation
   → [Rent modal on day 3 or 6]
   → Next morning  OR  Ending (day 7 / bankrupt)
```

**Per-day phases:**

```
1. MORNING       Owl delivers a reputation-tiered letter (typewriter reveal).
                 On rent days, a landlord reminder follows the main letter.

2. READ          Customer dossier revealed. Player distils it into a
                 3-entry memo (Purpose / Personality / Element) by clicking
                 content words in the prose and committing them to slots.
                 Memo gates Proceed and replaces the full dossier in later
                 scenes (the wrong memo will mislead later steps).

3. SHOP          AI generates 6 materials (3 cores + 3 woods) tailored —
                 but not perfectly matched — to the customer. Player spends
                 gold to buy at least 1 core and 1 wood. ✦ glyphs hint
                 cards whose affinity overlaps the memo.
                 (GDD goal: 1 commission/day at 1.75× — not yet implemented.)

4. CRAFT         Player assigns up to 2 cores + exactly 1 wood in
                 CraftingScene. Confirming the picks stashes them on
                 GameManager and loads MinigameTest.

5. RITUAL        MinigameTest hosts the 3-round tracing ritual AND the
                 wand-generation pipeline (OpenAI + ComfyUI image) in
                 parallel. Both must finish before EvaluationScene loads.
                 Quality grade (A/B/C/F) is set by rounds-won.

6. EVALUATE      OpenAI compares wand to dossier; match score (0–100)
                 × quality multiplier (A=1.0 .. F=0.4) = gold/rep reward.
                 Theatrical reveal: banner → wand → scoreboard → grade
                 letter, with sparkle VFX + URP Bloom.

7. SETTLE/ADVANCE  On day 3 or day 6: rent modal. Pay → next day; can't
                   pay → Bankrupt ending (Early on day 3, Late on day 6).
                   Otherwise: AdvanceToNextDay() → Morning of day N+1.
                   At end of day 7: ending tier resolved by reputation.
```

## 4. Customer Dossier

Generated by OpenAI API (gpt-4o). **Currently 1 customer per day.** (GDD goal: 2/day; not yet implemented.) The customer's reputation tier was originally meant to be passed into the prompt to bias the kind of clientele that visits — also not yet implemented.

### Fields

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| `customerName` | A fantasy name fitting the character |
| `schoolOfMagic` | Their magical discipline (e.g. Hydromancy, Storm magic, Shadow magic) |
| `profession` | Their specific job, written to describe HOW they use their magic (not just a title) |
| `personality` | Exactly 3 traits — at least one in tension with the others |
| `request` | What they say out loud in the shop. Conversational, first person, describes a specific practical problem |
| `trueGoal` | What they actually want to achieve — more ambitious and specific than the request |
| `constraint` | Their magical limitation. Directly explains why the problem in the request happens. Activates at the worst possible moment for their job |

### Design Rules for Generation

- `schoolOfMagic` → `profession` → `request` → `trueGoal` → `constraint` must form a logical chain
- `request` describes a specific drawback of their school of magic in their profession context
- `trueGoal` includes the profession's core demand and shows why those demands are slightly in tension
- `constraint` is cruelest when it activates exactly when the customer needs control most

### Established Examples (used as few-shot prompts)

**Example 1 — Storm magic**
```
customerName:  Elyra Voss
schoolOfMagic: Storm magic
profession:    Assassin mage who uses lightning for the final blow
personality:   Meticulous, deeply self-doubting, quietly competitive
request:       "I need something discreet for my job. I want the wand to prevent
               flashing so I can conjure my magic without visible sign."
trueGoal:      To perform a high-stakes lightning spell quickly at the exact right
               moment without it being too visible
constraint:    Her magic surges unpredictably when she feels watched
```

**Example 2 — Shadow magic**
```
customerName:  Dorian Ashveil
schoolOfMagic: Shadow magic
profession:    Bounty hunter who uses shadow magic to track and corner targets
personality:   Calculating, emotionally detached, secretly paranoid
request:       "I need a wand that keeps my shadow spells from dispersing mid-chase.
               My bindings keep collapsing the moment my target starts running."
trueGoal:      To cast shadow binding spells reliably at full sprint without
               losing hold of the spell
constraint:    His magic destabilises when his concentration splits between
               moving and casting simultaneously
```

**Example 3 — Hydromancy**
```
customerName:  Sable Mirehn
schoolOfMagic: Hydromancy
profession:    Field medic who uses water magic to perform emergency healing
personality:   Outwardly calm, internally panicked, fiercely protective
request:       "I need a wand that lets me control the exact pressure of my water
               flow. Too much force and I damage the wound instead of closing it."
trueGoal:      To perform precise, high-speed healing on multiple patients in
               rapid succession without losing control of the flow
constraint:    Her water magic amplifies in intensity when she is emotionally
               distressed — exactly when she needs it most delicate
```

## 5. Materials System

OpenAI API generates 6 materials per day (3 cores, 3 woods), tailored but not perfectly matched to today's customers.

### Core (Monster Part) Fields

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| `name` | Name of the monster part (e.g. "Leviathan Scale") |
| `price` | 0–300 gold. Scales with monster rarity and yield per creature |
| `elementalAffinity` | Which school of magic this material resonates with |
| `attributes` | What this material does functionally |
| `special` | An additional interaction effect, often double-edged |
| `imagePrompt` | Auto-generated prompt for ComfyUI pixel art generation |

**Price logic for cores:**
- Common creatures, abundant yield (e.g. nixie finger bone): 50–120g
- Uncommon creatures or low-yield parts: 120–200g
- Rare creatures or unique single-yield parts (e.g. leviathan scale): 200–300g

### Wood Fields

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| `name` | Tree species (must be a real tree) |
| `price` | 0–300 gold. Scales with rarity of the tree |
| `personalityMatch` | The personality types this wood channels best |
| `attributes` | Grounded in real-world or folkloric properties of that tree species |
| `imagePrompt` | Auto-generated prompt for ComfyUI pixel art generation |

**Wood grounding principle:** All wood attributes must trace to real-world/folkloric properties of that tree species.

### Misdirection Design (Critical)

**For cores:**
- One is the optimal choice
- One is a tempting trap (looks right but worsens the customer's constraint)
- One has a useful attribute but wrong scale or application for the true goal

**For woods:**
- One is the correct match (suits the magic problem)
- One suits the customer's role but not their magic problem
- One sounds partially relevant but has a physical property that conflicts with what the customer's magic needs

### Established Material Examples (for Hydromancy / Field Medic customer)

**Cores:**
```
Selkie Heart       | 180g | Water | Deepens emotional resonance.
                   |      |       | Special: emotional state shapes spell behaviour
                   |      |       | → TRAP: worsens the constraint

Leviathan Scale    | 270g | Water / Deep pressure | Caps maximum output force.
                   |      | Special: reduces peak intensity by 30%
                   |      | → GOOD for request, but limits true goal ceiling

Nixie Finger Bone  | 95g  | Water / Precision | Fine motor control, close-range accuracy.
                   |      | Special: difficult to scale up in power
                   |      | → RIGHT tool, wrong scale of work
```

**Woods:**
```
Weeping Willow | 60g  | Intuitive, emotionally guided, adaptive
               |      | Bends under magical pressure. Water + healing associations.
               |      | → CORRECT answer

Elder Wood     | 110g | Protective, willing to sacrifice, life-and-death work
               |      | Healing + warding associations. Dual nature, unstable energy.
               |      | → Suits the ROLE, not the magic problem

Ashwood        | 75g  | Resilient, enduring, performs under sustained pressure
               |      | Very rigid. Reliable but unforgiving of error.
               |      | → Rigid wood conflicts with delicate magic need
```

## 8. Crafting System

### Material Selection (CraftingScene)

- Up to 2 cores (Core 1 required, Core 2 optional), exactly 1 wood (required)
- Selected materials appear in crafting slots. X button returns to inventory.
- Confirming the picks removes them from inventory, stashes them on `GameManager.chosenCore1/Core2/Wood`, and loads `MinigameTest`. The crafting result modal that used to live in this scene is gone — the ritual happens in the next scene.

---

### Tracing Minigame — The Crafting Ritual (MinigameTest)

Inspired by Hogwarts Legacy's spell-learning mechanic. Performed 3 times in succession (binding the core, shaping the wood, sealing the enchantment). Lives in its own scene `5.MinigameTest.unity`. `MinigameSceneRunner` orchestrates **two concurrent operations** on scene load:

1. The 3-round tracing ritual (visualised below).
2. The wand-generation pipeline — OpenAI wand JSON + ComfyUI pixel art — running in the background while the player is busy with the ritual.

Both must complete before the scene transitions to `EvaluationScene`. Running the slow API/image work in parallel with gameplay means the player never waits twice.

**Setup:**
- A glowing rune trail appears with a start point and end point. Each trail is drawn from an orchestral conductor's beat pattern (4/4, 3/4, 6/8, 5/4, 2/4, or a freeform expressive cue) so tracing feels like conducting the magic with your wand
- Gate markers sit on the trail, each displaying a randomized keyboard key. 5 gates per round, placed at the baton's ictus points (strong articulation instants where the conductor reverses direction)
- A red fill begins advancing from the start as soon as the round begins, creating a "chased" feeling — if red fills the entire trail before the player finishes, the round is lost
- When the round begins, the OS mouse cursor is warped to the trail's starting point so the player never loses precious seconds hunting for where to begin

**How to play:**
- The player traces the trail with the mouse. If the cursor stays within the tolerance zone (+N pixels from the path), the correctly traced portion fills blue
- When the cursor reaches a gate marker, the gate resolution rules depend on the gate type (see below). In all cases the gate blocks forward progress until cleared
- Drawing past a gate without clearing it is not allowed — the blue fill does not advance beyond an uncleared gate
- **Win:** Blue fill reaches the end before red fill reaches the end
- **Lose:** Red fill reaches the end of the trail first

**Three gate types (easy to learn, hard to master):**

| Type | Visual cue | How to clear | Failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tap** (amber) | Amber square + key letter | Press the shown key once | — (just press the key) |
| **Hold** (cyan) | Cyan square + green halo + inner green fill that grows | Hold the key for ~0.9 s until the inner fill reaches the edge of the marker, then release | Releasing too early resets the fill; player can retry in place. Late release is free but mist kept advancing |
| **Accent** (magenta) | Magenta square + protruding directional arrow-line (one of 8 compass points) + `↑↗→↘↓↙←↖` suffix on key label | Press the shown key, then flick the mouse ≥60 px in the shown direction (±30° tolerance), then return the mouse to the trail | Wrong-direction flicks do nothing; player can re-aim. No explicit fail — time pressure is the failure |

Crucially, **the red mist never pauses while resolving a gate.** Every hold and every accent is a commitment of time the player won't get back, so over-committing on specials means losing the round.

**Round escalation** (fixed — teaches one new mechanic per round):

| Round | Composition | New teaching |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5 Tap gates | Baseline tracing + key press + mist pressure |
| 2 | 4 Tap + 1 Hold (random position) | Introduces the time-commitment hold |
| 3 | 3 Tap + 1 Hold + 1 Accent (random different positions) | Full conducting — timing + directional gesture |

**Three rounds:**
Each round uses a different conducting pattern (3 of 6 picked uniquely per session). 5 gates land at the ictus points of each pattern. Fill speed stays constant at 0.12 normalized-units/sec (≈8.3s per round). Brief thematic transition between rounds. No retries — each round is a single win-or-lose attempt.

**Quality grading:**

| Grade | Condition | Reward multiplier |
|---|---|---|
| A | All 3 rounds won | 1.0× |
| B | 2 rounds won | 0.85× |
| C | 1 round won | 0.7× |
| F | 0 rounds won | 0.4× |

Each win adds +1 to the cumulative success count. Grade A should feel earned: achievable but not trivial.

### Wand Generation Pipeline (concurrent with the minigame)

While the player is tracing, `MinigameSceneRunner.WandPipeline` does:

1. OpenAI API call generates the wand result based on selected materials.
2. ComfyUI generates a 128×128 pixel art image of the wand.
3. The result is stashed on `GameManager.currentWandResult`.

`MinigameSceneRunner.TryAdvance()` waits on both `_pipelineDone` and `_minigameDone` flags before loading `EvaluationScene`. The grade letter and rounds-won surface in the evaluation reveal alongside the score.

**OpenAI wand generation prompt rules:**
- Wand properties must emerge from the combination — not list inputs separately
- If two cores: reason how their affinities interact (amplify, conflict, or create something unexpected)
- Wood personality shapes HOW magic is channelled, not what the magic does
- Every sentence must trace back to a specific property of a specific material
- `wandName` must be evocative and specific (e.g. "Tidebreaker", "Ashbone Whisperer" — never "Wand of Power")
- `attributes`: exactly 3 short lines, synthesised from how materials interact — not copied from input
- `imagePrompt`: wand object only, no characters, no hands, no background. Must end with: "pixel art, 128x128, transparent background, centered, highly detailed, item icon style"

## 9. Evaluation System

OpenAI API compares wand against customer dossier and returns:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| `matchScore` | 0–100 |
| `verdict` | 2–3 sentences, senior wandmaker journal voice |
| `whatWorked` | 1–2 sentences |
| `whatMissed` | 1–2 sentences (null if score ≥ 85) |
| `customerReaction` | What the customer says on receiving the wand, first person |

**Scoring rules given to AI:**
- Addresses request only → 40–60
- Addresses true goal → 60–75
- Addresses true goal AND accounts for constraint → 75–95
- Resolves tension between true goal and constraint creatively → 90–100
- Conflicts with constraint despite matching request → 20–40

### Quality Grade Modifier (scales reward, not matchScore)

| Grade | Condition | Reward multiplier |
|---|---|---|
| A | 3/3 rounds won | 1.0× (full reward) |
| B | 2/3 rounds won | 0.85× |
| C | 1/3 rounds won | 0.7× |
| D | (reserved — currently unreachable from `ComputeGrade`) | 0.55× |
| F | 0/3 rounds won | 0.4× |

`EvaluationManager.GetQualityMultiplier` accepts 'D' for forward compatibility with future grading rubrics; `TracingMinigameUI.ComputeGrade` only emits A/B/C/F today.

### Reward Calculation

```
baseGold       = 150 * (matchScore / 100)
baseReputation = matchScore >= 40 ? 20 * (matchScore / 100) : -10

goldEarned     = Mathf.RoundToInt(baseGold * qualityMultiplier)
repEarned      = Mathf.RoundToInt(baseReputation * qualityMultiplier)
```

### Evaluation Scene Layout (UI Toolkit)

`EvaluationResultController` drives a theatrical reveal sequence rather than a static three-column layout:

1. **Banner** — customer name + school of magic.
2. **Wand** — pixel-art image fades in, name + description scroll.
3. **Scoreboard** — Conjuring (rounds-won), Materials, Customer Fit, Reward (gold/rep).
4. **Final Letter Grade** — A/B/C/F surfaced with a cinematic stamp.
5. **Companion VFX** — `WandSparkles` ParticleSystem + `PostFX Volume` (URP Bloom).

Day-progress dots are still computed but no longer the focus of the screen. On rent days (3, 6) `RentPaymentUI` modal appears before the day advances. "Next Customer" routes through `EvaluationManager.OnNextCustomer()` which either advances to the next morning, opens the rent modal, or routes to the ending depending on day/gold state.

## 10. Progression

A session is a fixed 7-day arc with two rent checkpoints and a multi-ending payoff at the end of day 7.

### Rent schedule

| Day | Rent | Failure |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 250g | `BankruptEarly` ending |
| 6 | 400g | `BankruptLate` ending |

### Ending tiers (resolved at end of day 7 from `playerReputation`)

| Tier | Threshold | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **Royal** | rep ≥ 90 (`ROYAL_REP_MIN`) | Crown summons. |
| **Rival** | 30 ≤ rep < 90 (`RIVAL_REP_MIN`) | A copycat opens across the street. Bittersweet. |
| **Slum** | rep < 30 | Drummed out of town; shady alley wands. |

Plus two bankruptcy variants (`BankruptEarly` if it hit on day 3, `BankruptLate` if day 6) — total of **5 endings**. See `Docs/SevenDayProgression.md` for the full system.

Per-day reputation delta is −10 to +20 (`EvaluationManager.RewardCalculation`), so Royal needs roughly 5 strong days. Reputation tiers were originally meant to bias the customer-generation prompt (e.g. higher rep → richer clientele) — **not yet implemented**; the prompt is currently static.

## 16. Memo-Gated Reading

The dossier is no longer a paragraph the player skims. After generation, `DossierPanelController` (UI Toolkit) renders the prose with content words wrapped as clickable links; the player must commit one keyword each into three slots — **Purpose** (from `request`/`trueGoal`), **Personality** (from `personality`/`profession`), **Element** (from `schoolOfMagic`) — before Proceed enables. The completed `PlayerMemo` then *replaces* the full dossier in market and crafting scenes, and matching keywords drive ✦ glyphs on cards whose affinity overlaps the memo. A wrong memo actively misleads downstream choices — the gate is soft (any content word in a valid source field is accepted), so the player's interpretation is what they carry forward.

Full design + verification: `Docs/MemoFeature.md`.

## 17. 7-Day Progression & Endings

Sessions run a fixed 7 days bookended by Title and Ending scenes. Each morning opens with a reputation-tiered letter (`LetterLibrary`, 21 hand-authored entries across 8 sender archetypes) typed out via `TypewriterText`. Rent is billed on days 3 (250g) and 6 (400g) via the `RentPaymentUI` modal on the evaluation screen — Pay or Plead both succeed if you can afford it; otherwise "Accept fate" routes to a Bankrupt ending. End of day 7 routes to one of Royal / Rival / Slum based on `playerReputation` against `ROYAL_REP_MIN = 90` / `RIVAL_REP_MIN = 30`. Plus two bankruptcy variants by trigger day. `EndingManager` loads a pre-generated illustration from `Resources/EndingArt/`, fades it in, types out hand-written dialogue, and shows session stats.

Full design + status: `Docs/SevenDayProgression.md`.

## 18. UI Toolkit Migration

The project is mid-migration from uGUI (Canvas + RectTransform + Image/TMP) to **UI Toolkit** (UIDocument + UXML + USS). Pattern: one `*UIDocument` GameObject per scene with a UXML source and a controller MonoBehaviour. Legacy uGUI Canvas children are kept but disabled so reverts are cheap.

Folder layout: `Assets/UI/<feature>/<feature>Panel.{uxml,uss}` plus a `<feature>PanelSettings.asset`.

| TitleScene | ✅ UI Toolkit (`TitleScreenController`) |
| MorningScene | ✅ UI Toolkit (`MorningScreenController`) |
| CustomerGeneratorTest | ✅ UI Toolkit (`DossierPanelController`) |
| MaterialGeneratorTest | 🟡 UXML/USS authored, no UIDocument GO yet (still uGUI driver) |
| CraftingScene | ✅ UI Toolkit (`CraftingWorkbenchUI`) |
