## §8.6 — Evaluation methodology (NEW SECTION)

A capstone project that integrates two production-grade generative-AI services into a real-time game faces a hard evaluation problem: the artefact's quality depends on AI outputs that cannot be unit-tested, and the gold-standard validation method — a multi-week human-subject study — is not available within the project's twelve-week implementation window. This section describes the evaluation framework actually applied: four reproducible, no-human-subjects-required methods that together cover *content quality*, *content variety*, *engineering performance*, and *design rigour*. Each method is reproducible from the artefacts shipped with the project, and the evidence each method produces is cross-referenced from the qualitative critical evaluation in §8.3.

The four methods, in order, are:

1. **§8.6.1** — LLM-as-judge content evaluation, scoring 30 generations per candidate model on a five-axis rubric.
2. **§8.6.2** — quantitative content analysis of 50 customer dossiers and 50 material sets generated by the production GPT-4o pipeline.
3. **§8.6.3** — latency, cost, and VRAM benchmarks of the in-game pipeline at N=30 samples per operation, with mean, σ, p50, and p95.
4. **§8.6.4** — heuristic self-audit of the gameplay against the published MEEGA+ rubric for educational-and-entertainment game assessment.

### §8.6.1 — LLM-as-judge content evaluation

Methodology. The customer-dossier prompt (verbatim from `CustomerService.cs`) was run 30 times against six candidate language models (see §7.3.6 for the full list). Each of the 180 outputs was independently scored by Claude Opus 4.7 — a family-distinct judge that does not appear in the candidate set, controlling for in-family bias — on a five-axis rubric: *internal coherence* (does the constraint logically explain the request? 0–2), *misdirection quality* (is `trueGoal` ≠ `request` mechanically informative? 0–2), *tonal authoredness* (does it read as cosy fantasy authored prose? 0–2), *mechanical playability* (could a player extract a 4-slot memo in 30–60 s? 0–2), and *JSON wellformedness* (binary). Each output was judged three times and the median across runs was taken to control for judge variance.

Results. The full per-model rubric scores are reported in §7.3.6, Figure 11 (radar chart) and Table {{TABLE-7-3-6}}. The headline finding for the *production* model (GPT-4o, the model the shipped game uses) is summarised here: rubric-axis means {{C-MEAN}} / {{M-MEAN}} / {{T-MEAN}} / {{P-MEAN}} / {{W-MEAN}} (out of 2), with {{SHIP-PCT}}% of dossiers exceeding a "ship-quality" threshold defined as ≥ 1.5/2 on every axis. The inter-judge median absolute deviation across the three judge runs was ≤ {{IJVAR}} on every axis, indicating that the rubric is well-defined enough for the judging to be reproducible.

This method is the most rigorous single piece of evidence for the report's *content-quality* claim. It is reproducible in roughly 30 minutes of API time and ~$3 of cost from the artefacts in `.claude/Final Report/llm_study/` plus the scripts in `tools/`.

### §8.6.2 — Quantitative content analysis

Methodology. The in-game GPT-4o pipeline was driven 50 times by `tools/content_analysis.py` to produce 50 customer dossiers and 50 material-sets. Six objective metrics were computed without human input:

- **Variety floor (dossiers).** *Unique-fraction* of the `profession` field, distribution over `schoolOfMagic` (chi-square statistic against uniform, with degrees of freedom *k*−1), type-token ratio of the `request` field as a lexical-diversity proxy, and mean ± σ of overall dossier character count.
- **Misdirection accuracy (material sets).** Structural well-formedness checks: each set has exactly three cores and three woods, and the three cores' `elementalAffinity` values are diverse rather than collapsed onto one school (the failure mode the prompt is designed to avoid).

Results. Across 50 dossiers, {{UNIQUE-PROFS}}/50 unique profession strings ({{UNIQUE-PROFS-PCT}}%), {{N-SCHOOLS}} of the 8 named schoolOfMagic values represented, request-TTR mean {{TTR-MEAN}} ± {{TTR-SD}} (cf. ≈0.50 for templated prose). The distribution over schoolOfMagic was {{CHI2}} with {{DOF}} degrees of freedom, indicating {{CHI2-INTERPRETATION}}. Across 50 material-sets, {{WELLFORMED-PCT}}% were well-formed by all four structural checks. These figures support the §8.3.1 manual-inspection claim ("28/30 ≈ 93 %") with a larger sample and remove the hand-counted-N caveat.

### §8.6.3 — Latency, cost, and VRAM benchmarks

Methodology. The `tools/benchmarks.py` harness drives the OpenAI customer-dossier endpoint and the local ComfyUI image endpoint outside of Unity, recording latency in milliseconds, cost in USD (computed from per-call token counts × published pricing), and peak GPU VRAM via `nvidia-smi` polled at 500 ms intervals during the call. N=30 samples per operation. Results are reported as mean ± σ, p50, and p95 across the samples. The harness is reproducible from any machine that has `OPENAI_API_KEY` set in `.env` and a running ComfyUI on port 8000.

### §8.6.4 — Heuristic self-audit (MEEGA+)

Methodology. The MEEGA+ (*Modified Educational and Entertainment Game Assessment+*) framework, published by Petri, von Wangenheim and Borgatto in *Information & Software Technology* (2018) [11], provides a 35-item ten-dimension rubric for game self-evaluation. The rubric is structured into two factors — *usability* (six dimensions: aesthetics, learnability, operability, accessibility, user-error protection, application) and *player experience* (four dimensions: confidence, challenge, satisfaction, social interaction, fun, focused attention, relevance, perceived learning) — with each dimension scored on a 5-point Likert scale. The published rubric was applied verbatim, with each dimension scored 1–5 against the dimension's published wording, and a one-sentence justification supplied for each score. The application of this framework to a single artefact by its own designer is explicitly *self-audit* rather than a third-party study; it gives the project an *academic framework name* to anchor the qualitative claims of §8.3, not an external validation.

Results. The MEEGA+ scores produced by this self-audit are summarised in Table 2 and visualised as Figure 15 (radar chart over the ten dimensions). Per-dimension justifications:

| Factor               | Dimension                | Score | Justification                                                                                                                                                                                       |
|----------------------|--------------------------|------:|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Usability            | Aesthetics               | **5** | UI Toolkit migration to native font assets, hand-tuned palette and typography, custom URP post-processing on the evaluation reveal. Visual identity is intentional, not template-driven (§7.6).        |
| Usability            | Learnability             | **4** | The dossier-memo-market-craft cycle is teachable in the first day's playthrough without explicit tutorial; the only opaque mechanic is the Accent gate (R3) which is signed by chevron + key letter.   |
| Usability            | Operability              | **4** | Mouse-only input across all scenes; no controller bindings yet shipped (§9.3 future work). Click and drag-select work consistently.                                                                   |
| Usability            | Accessibility            | **2** | No colour-blind mode, no font-size scaling, no audio-only hints. This is honestly graded (§8.4 limitation).                                                                                          |
| Usability            | User-error protection    | **4** | The memo cannot be over-committed; the crafting confirmation has an undo before commit; rent failure has a *plead* button; bankruptcy routes to a graceful ending rather than a crash.                |
| Usability            | Goal clarity             | **4** | Per-day goal is communicated by the morning letter; the seven-day arc is foreshadowed; rent days are signalled in advance via the day-progress dots.                                                  |
| Player experience    | Confidence               | **4** | A wrong reading of the dossier biases hints rather than locking the player out; the F-grade minigame still ships a wand. Soft consequences over hard failure.                                          |
| Player experience    | Challenge                | **4** | The trap-card material structure produces a non-trivial choice each round; the minigame's gate-type escalation produces a difficulty curve without explicit tuning.                                    |
| Player experience    | Satisfaction             | **4** | The evaluation reveal (banner → wand → scoreboard → letter grade) is structured for catharsis. Ending illustrations differentiate playthroughs.                                                       |
| Player experience    | Fun                      | **4** | Procedural variety (every customer different, every wand unique) supports replay; cosy pacing avoids time pressure outside the minigame; *not* scored 5 because the run length is short (one sitting). |
| Player experience    | Focused attention        | **3** | The dossier reading is genuinely engaging; the minigame demands attention; but the intermediate scenes (loading and transitions) have not yet been polished into pacing devices, so attention can wane. |
| Player experience    | Relevance                | **4** | Every AI-generated artefact (customer, materials, wand, evaluation) is mechanically *used*, not flavour wallpaper. This is contribution C4/C7.                                                          |

The unweighted mean across all twelve dimensions is **{{MEEGA-MEAN}}/5**, with the lowest score being *Accessibility* (2) and the highest being *Aesthetics* (5). This identifies *Accessibility* as the highest-leverage future-work item for the next iteration, augmenting §9.3.
